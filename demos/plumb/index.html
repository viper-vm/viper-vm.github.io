      How Plumb works

      2D drawings hide what's above and below.

      Floating columns, toilets over bedrooms, soil stacks that jog, lift wells that drift a few centimetres — they live between sheets, so nobody sees them until the site does. BIM clash detection needs a 3D model. Plumb works from the 2D plans you already have.

      1. Reads your drawings — DWG (AutoCAD R13 to 2025) or DXF (ASCII or binary, any version): every floor plan in one file (side by side, each titled like “FIRST FLOOR PLAN”), or one file per floor, in any mix.
      2. Understands the drawing — works out units, which layers are walls, columns, doors, windows, ducts and stairs, and finds every room from the linework, naming it from the text inside. Open-plan spaces are split by their labels.
      3. Stacks the floors — aligns each floor on the one below by matching columns (outliers are exactly the columns that don't line up).
      4. Checks what lines up — columns with nothing below, columns that step sideways or grow, wet rooms over bedrooms and living rooms, ducts, stairs and lifts that don't continue, and slabs that cantilever beyond the floor below.
      5. Hands it back — a markups DXF you can XREF over your drawing, a CSV and a printable report.

      Three views. Overlay puts each floor over the one below (the ruler button shows the dimensions drawn in the file). Stack pulls the floors apart to show what continues. Model turns the plans into a 3D building — walls to full height, doors, windows, slabs, parapets and a roof — which you can cut through floor by floor, set the storey heights for, and download as .glb.

      Optional Claude assist. With your own Anthropic API key, Ask Claude reads layer names and room labels that rules miss (office codes, M.B.R., T&B, labels in Hindi or German…) and suggests corrections you approve; RFIs drafts a request for each consultant from the findings. Only names and findings are sent — never the drawing.

      Plumb is a coordination aid, not a structural design check — every issue is a question to put to your engineer. The analysis runs entirely in your browser; drawings never leave your computer. Shortcuts: 2 overlay, 3 stack, m model, d dimensions, j/k step through issues, f fits, Esc clears. DWG files are read with LibreDWG (GPL), downloaded the first time you open one.
